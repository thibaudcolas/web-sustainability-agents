## Priorities

### 1. Check when the shared TateDraw image is needed

<div class="audit-finding" markdown="1">

**High priority** · Needs investigation · <a class="audit-guideline" href="https://www.w3.org/TR/web-sustainability-guidelines/#optimize-media-to-reduce-resource-use" title="Include media only when it supports user needs. Manage, optimize, and load media efficiently. Ensure users have control over playback and data use.">WSG 2.9: Optimize media to reduce resource use</a>

**What we found.** A roughly 859 kB TateDraw JPG is fetched across the sampled templates, including homepage and greener-website states where its promotion is hidden or offscreen. The evidence establishes a useful investigation target; it does not yet establish a safe change.

**Why it matters.** A shared request can affect visitors arriving on many different pages. Checking this promotion’s activation offers a focused way to investigate avoidable initial transfer while preserving useful art and navigation.

**Next step.** Trace the request to the shared promotion, open it with pointer and keyboard controls, and test loading a suitable rendition only when needed. Check image detail at intended screen densities and compare matched complete visits before claiming savings.

[How we checked this: Check when the shared TateDraw image is needed](#evidence-media-delivery)

</div>

### 2. Keep the cookie inventory readable without JavaScript

<div class="audit-finding" markdown="1">

**Medium priority** · Problem found · <a class="audit-guideline" href="https://www.w3.org/TR/web-sustainability-guidelines/#provide-cross-platform-compatibility-support" title="Document compatibility, avoid unnecessary obsolescence, and test across devices, systems, and network conditions.">WSG 2.17: Provide cross-platform compatibility support</a>

**What we found.** The cookie policy loses its vendor/cookie list and Manage Preferences material when JavaScript is unavailable. Other sampled reading content remains accessible, but no equivalent policy alternative is provided for this missing information.

**Why it matters.** Visitors with unavailable or failed scripts cannot inspect the same cookie details or find the same preference material.

**Next step.** Render the inventory in HTML or link an equivalent static policy and usable preference-management alternative. Confirm a fresh visit without JavaScript can read the policy information and reach the alternative.

[How we checked this: Keep the cookie inventory readable without JavaScript](#evidence-no-js-content)

</div>

### 3. Compress the consent-provider script and document

<div class="audit-finding" markdown="1">

**Low priority** · Problem found · <a class="audit-guideline" href="https://www.w3.org/TR/web-sustainability-guidelines/#reduce-data-transfer-with-compression" title="Compress server responses and media files to reduce bandwidth, storage use, and delivery overhead while balancing processing costs.">WSG 4.3: Reduce data transfer with compression</a>

**What we found.** On What's on, consenTag.js and popup_silent.html return successful text responses without a content-encoding, at roughly 6.6 kB and 12.0 kB. Core Tate scripts and styles are already compressed.

**Why it matters.** These specific text responses are candidates for smaller transfers through beneficial compression. The achievable change and its effect on energy have not been measured.

**Next step.** Ask the provider to enable compression through content negotiation. Verify the delivered encoding and preference-manager behavior, then measure a matched visit to establish the transfer change.

[How we checked this: Compress the consent-provider script and document](#evidence-text-compression)

</div>

### 4. Explain how the historical website estimates compare

<div class="audit-finding" markdown="1">

**Low priority** · Needs investigation · <a class="audit-guideline" href="https://www.w3.org/TR/web-sustainability-guidelines/#define-clear-organizational-sustainability-goals-and-metrics" title="Define measurable sustainability goals and metrics, and communicate progress over time.">WSG 5.6: Define clear organizational sustainability goals and metrics</a>

**What we found.** Tate reports website estimates of 45 metric tons CO₂e in 2019 and 27 metric tons in 2023. Its website-specific statement and Digital Green Team provide useful context, but the captured evidence does not document comparable calculation methods and boundaries.

**Why it matters.** Readers need to know whether changes in traffic, scope or calculation methods explain part of the difference before treating it as like-for-like progress. These historical estimates also use a different basis from this audit’s current scenarios.

**Next step.** Publish a concise method note for both years covering service boundary, traffic, model inputs and any recalculations. Add one dated review showing how the team used a result to choose its next action.

[How we checked this: Explain how the historical website estimates compare](#evidence-sustainability-goals)

</div>

## Next steps for auditing

1. Trace and test the shared TateDraw promotion before changing its loading; preserve image quality and accessible activation.
2. Provide an HTML cookie inventory and usable preference alternative, then test the fresh no-JavaScript policy-reading task and the actual privacy choices.
3. Recheck the skip link after focus and screen layout settle, exercise narrow controls, and inspect the recorded reduced-motion transitions.
4. Complete the missing transfer coverage and repeat any changed page under matching conditions. Keep annual scenarios separate from historical claims and browser-energy modeling.
5. Publish comparable methods for the 2019/2023 website estimates, one dated review-to-action example and corroborated hosting evidence.

The sample covers selected pages and visitor tasks, not every page on the site. Expand it where the checks above identify an untested template, interaction or audience need.

## Methodology and reproducibility

The browser measurements were captured from **2026-11-06T08:48:24.435889+00:00** to **2026-11-06T08:50:07.454109+00:00**. Initial-load tests used a 1280 × 800 desktop viewport and a 3-second observation window. Cookie choices and scrolling were recorded afterward. Supplementary tests covered narrow layouts, color and motion preferences, print, JavaScript availability and bounded keyboard navigation.

**Audit judgments:** Codex native subagent, gpt-6.1-sol, **high reasoning**. **Report wording:** Codex native subagent, gpt-6.1-sol, **high reasoning**.

The scoring method is `agent-breadth-v3`. A guideline group counts once even if it has several checks. Observed problems take precedence over unresolved checks in that group. 0 groups were excluded as not applicable; 44 were untested. Unknowns remain in the passing-percentage denominator; untested and inapplicable groups do not. Area percentages use the same rule as the overall score.

### First load {#measure-first-load}

Data downloaded before clicking or scrolling, using the first recorded initial visit for each URL, including its completeness limit. Decimal kB = 1,000 bytes; MB = 1,000,000 bytes.

### Cookie choices and waiting {#measure-after-choice}

No cookie banner was observed in this sample. The intermediate checkpoint waits without clicking. Later columns show **cumulative totals**, including the initial load of that visit. They are not additional amounts to add together or guaranteed savings.

### Scrolling {#measure-scroll}

Cumulative downloads after scrolling to the bottom, following the cookie-choice or waiting checkpoint of the same visit. Compare accept and reject values within their own visit; do not subtract the first-load headline from a different visit.

### Partial measurements {#measure-completeness}

A partial result means some requests failed or did not finish in the observation window, so their transferred size is uncertain. The site is measured as it behaved; a failed analytics request is context, not automatically a priority fix. Failed document loads and HTTP error responses are shown as unavailable, since their bytes do not represent the requested page. Carbon estimates and ratings are withheld for failed or incomplete initial captures. Unmarked transfer figures are complete within the test conditions, not a measure of every possible download.

### Estimated footprint and Digital Carbon Rating {#measure-carbon}

Both use the same initial visit as the first-load column. Footprints are estimates in grams of carbon dioxide equivalent (gCO₂e) per first visit. They display two decimal places, with a minimum of 0.01 gCO₂e for positive estimates. Digital Carbon Ratings use unrounded estimates from the fixed comparison model. Recorded model: Sustainable Web Design Model version 4 through `@tgwf/co2@0.19.0`, assuming 100% first visits. The comparison gives no green-hosting credit. These are model assumptions, not verified electricity-supply facts. Ratings remain provisional because the capture protocol has not been independently validated; they do not establish whole-site sustainability.

### Browser and network energy {#measure-energy}

Energy is shown in milliwatt-hours (mWh = 0.001 Wh). Browser energy includes modeled CPU, memory, disk and display use. Not sampled means no energy experiment was supplied for that page; unavailable means a supplied experiment did not yield a usable result. Neither means zero energy.

**Energy method:** GreenFrame 2.0.1 uses separately recorded browser scenarios, with three fresh-context visits per page, page load followed by a three-second wait, and no cookie interaction or scrolling. The median client-device estimate and median network estimate are reported separately; their medians must not be added to derive a total. This is a resource-use model, not metered whole-device electricity. Browser startup and the collector warm-up are excluded; browser close is included.

The pinned container uses Chromium 110 and a fixed Chrome 89 user-agent, independently of the transfer collector. The model assumes a 14 W display; that assumption contributes substantially to these short visits. CPU, memory and disk estimates use the collection host's properties. Background activity on this shared workstation was not independently isolated. These figures support repeat experiments under the same conditions, not device-independent rankings. GreenFrame network energy and SWDM carbon estimates overlap in scope and are not added together.

Illustrations were captured separately from the transfer measurements. Their capture dates, viewports, URLs and file hashes are retained in the screenshot manifest. No source checkout was included in this audit.

### Annual footprint {#measure-annual}

**Traffic:** CrUX dataset month `2026-09`, exact origin `https://www.tate.org.uk`, popularity bucket 100,000. The [CrUX popularity metric](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/crux/methodology/metrics#popularity) is a coarse navigation-based rank, not a pageview count. The [rank-to-pageviews mapping](https://github.com/thibaudcolas/ranking-to-pageviews) applies medians calibrated on US government sites using 2025 analytics and December 2025 ranks. This population and period differ from this site. No calibrated site-level uncertainty interval is available; these are scenarios, not measured analytics or a forecast.

**SWDM v4:** Annual pageviews × the arithmetic mean of unrounded emissions per sampled URL ÷ 1,000 = kgCO₂e/year. Use the first recorded initial visit per URL, equal page weights and 100% first visits. The actual mix of pages and returning visitors is unknown. [SWDM v4](https://sustainablewebdesign.org/estimating-digital-emissions/) covers modeled operational and embodied browsing emissions across devices, networks and data centers; it is not a complete inventory of this site's backend, builds or organization.

Partial transfer inputs can support this explicitly labeled scenario, but do not establish a complete footprint or qualify for a Digital Carbon Rating. Ratings remain withheld where initial captures are incomplete.

**Client-energy projection:** The 2 energy-sampled URLs out of 7 audited URLs receive equal weights. Take each URL's median of three matching GreenFrame runs, average those unrounded medians, and multiply by estimated pageviews ÷ 1,000 to convert Wh to annual kWh. This assumes every pageview repeats that initial-load desktop scenario; it does not cover later reading, scrolling, other devices, network energy, remote servers or embodied emissions.

Convert client kWh to operational kgCO₂e using **494 gCO₂e/kWh** ÷ 1,000. This is an explicit comparison assumption, using the SWDM v4 default unless overridden; the audience's electricity mix is unknown. GreenFrame estimates energy from observed resource use, not an electricity meter. Its fixed display/CPU coefficients and short laboratory duration can dominate these values. Do not add either client projection to SWDM or substitute it for SWDM's entire device segment.

### Potential improvement scale {#measure-annual-savings}

The illustrative reduction equals each annual estimate × 0.10 at unchanged traffic. It assumes that average per-view impact falls by 10% across the modeled population; it does not establish that any recommended fix will achieve this. A 10% byte reduction is not automatically a 10% reduction in measured client energy. Validate a change with matched before/after scenarios, then apply its measured difference only to the affected share of annual pageviews. Baseline device/display activity is not automatically removable by site changes.

All annual figures scale linearly with the traffic assumption: half as many pageviews halves them; twice as many doubles them. Replace the rank proxy with matching-origin analytics, page weights, repeat-visit proportions and audience device/electricity data to improve the estimate. Sample minimum/maximum intensities in the artifact are sensitivity cases, not confidence bounds.
